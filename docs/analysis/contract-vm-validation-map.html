7. What stays true, what to watch

Limits that remain after v1.8.0.

  • The address is bound, not its code. A contract that changes its behaviour later keeps the key.
  • A contract that executes arbitrary calls can be validated by anyone, once a controller names it.
  • A DID must keep one EOA authentication key; the seven writes need one.
  • A deactivated DID with no controller and no live key cannot come back.
  • A controller's deactivation is not final while the DID has a live key of its own.
  • The validator picks the key lifetime, up to the largest value; an open-call contract lets a stranger pick it.
  • A pending key cannot be withdrawn and can be validated later, even on a deactivated DID.

8. Decisions and not covered

Both decisions are taken (owner, 2026-11-17).

D5 decided, B: the DID's own live key can reactivate

Decided: B. The self path checks a live key only; the controller rule applies on the controller path only. A controller's deactivation is not final while the DID has a live key of its own.

D6 decided, A: such a DID cannot come back

Decided: A. Accepted and documented: a deactivated DID with no controller whose keys have all expired is stuck. The alternative keeps a way back in, which is the behaviour being removed.

Not covered by this design

  • ERC-6492 (wallets not yet deployed): unchanged, not supported.
  • Letting a contract perform DID writes: the seven document writes stay direct-EOA.
  • A bound on the expiration argument of validateVm: existing behaviour, listed as a finding.
